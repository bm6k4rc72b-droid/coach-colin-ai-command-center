What you're actually looking at is not fat

Before you change a single thing, you need to know which dial you are turning. Four systems are responsible for almost all of the softness people blame on body fat — and none of them take weeks to move.

Fat loss is slow arithmetic. A genuine pound of body fat is roughly 3,500 calories of deficit, and nobody loses four of them over a long weekend. Yet clients routinely look dramatically different in seventy-two hours, and the scale routinely moves three to six pounds in that window. That is not a contradiction and it is not a trick. It is water, intestinal gas, stool transit, and glycogen — four highly responsive systems that most people never address because they are too busy attacking calories.

On a GLP-1 medication this matters more, not less. The medication is doing the hard part for you: it slows gastric emptying, blunts appetite signalling, and pulls your intake down. What it does not do is manage the downstream consequences of eating less, drinking less, moving less food through the tube, and unintentionally stripping the minerals and fibre your gut needs to stay flat and regular. That gap is where the bloat lives. That gap is what this protocol closes.

Dial 01 — Mineral ratio

Sodium, potassium, and the rebound trap

Your body defends fluid balance with aldosterone. Slash sodium to zero and aldosterone climbs to protect you, so the moment you eat normally again you retain harder than before. The lever is not less salt — it is more potassium alongside sensible salt, which shifts fluid out of the space under your skin and back into circulation where it can be cleared.

Immediate actionKeep salt reasonable. Add three potassium-dense foods per day.

Dial 02 — Motility

Transit time is a cosmetic variable

Slowed gastric emptying is the mechanism that makes GLP-1 medication work — and the same mechanism means food and gas sit longer. Add reduced fibre and reduced fluid, and transit slows further. The visible result is lower abdominal distension that has nothing to do with adipose tissue and everything to do with volume in the pipe.

Immediate actionTen-minute walk after every meal. No exceptions, no negotiation.

Dial 03 — Cortisol & sleep

Stress is a fluid-retention hormone

Short sleep, alcohol, travel, and grinding stress all raise the hormones that tell your kidneys to hold water — and they blunt the gut barrier at the same time. This is why you can eat perfectly all week, sleep five hours for two nights, and wake up looking puffier than when you were eating badly.

Immediate actionFixed wake time for three days. Zero alcohol for seventy-two hours.

Dial 04 — Microbiome

Gas is a fermentation output

Bloat is frequently gas, and gas is what happens when gut bacteria ferment what reaches them. Sugar alcohols, carbonation, swallowed air, and a sudden fibre flood are the four fastest ways to manufacture it. Most people trying to "eat healthier" accidentally do all four at once and conclude their body is broken.

Immediate actionCut carbonation and sugar alcohols entirely for the full seventy-two hours.

You are not going to lose four pounds of fat this weekend. You are going to stop wearing three pounds of water and gas that were never yours to carry. Those are two completely different projects, and only one of them finishes by Monday.

Unorthodox nutrition — the seven rules

Every one of these will feel backwards compared to what you have been told. That is the point. Conventional advice is written for people who are not on a medication that halves their appetite and slows their stomach.

  1. RULE 01

    Stop cutting salt. Start chasing potassium.

    The instinct when you feel puffy is to strip sodium out. It backfires. Aggressive sodium restriction triggers a hormonal defence that makes you hold fluid harder the moment normal eating resumes, and on a GLP-1 medication — where total intake is already low — it can leave you genuinely under-replaced, dizzy, and headachy.

    Instead, run the ratio. Keep salt at a normal, sane level and deliberately build potassium up around it: avocado, spinach, plain potato, white beans, salmon, cantaloupe, plain yoghurt. Three servings a day, every day, for three days. Potassium is the mineral that helps shift fluid out of the space just under your skin — the exact space that decides how you look.

  2. RULE 02

    Eat in order: protein, then colour, then starch.

    Not what you eat — the sequence you eat it in. Leading with protein and fibrous vegetables before the starch changes the shape of the glucose curve after that meal and stacks your body's own satiety signalling on top of what the medication is already doing. Same plate, same calories, different physiological result.

    It also solves the most common failure I see: people on GLP-1 medication fill up after four bites, and if those four bites were bread, they just ate their entire meal's worth of nothing. Protein first means the four bites that actually land are the four that count.

  3. RULE 03

    The four-hour finish line.

    Last calorie of the day goes in four hours before your head hits the pillow. Not three. Four. This single rule does more for how flat you look at 7am than anything else on this page.

    Two reasons. First, your gut runs a cleanout wave between meals — a sweeping contraction pattern that only operates when you are genuinely empty, and it needs uninterrupted hours to do its job. Second, with delayed gastric emptying, lying down on a partly-full stomach is a direct route to overnight reflux, disturbed sleep, and a morning that starts distended.

  4. RULE 04

    Climb the fibre ladder. Never flood it.

    Here is the mistake almost everyone makes: they realise they are constipated, they panic, and on day one they eat forty grams of fibre. Twelve hours later they are in visible pain and convinced they have an intolerance. They don't — they force-fed a bacterial population that wasn't staffed for it.

    Add five grams per day, maximum, and lead with soluble fibre — psyllium, chia, oats, kiwifruit, cooked-and-cooled potato. Soluble fibre softens and moves; a wall of raw insoluble fibre on a slow stomach mostly just sits there and ferments. Every gram of added fibre needs added water or you have built a plug, not a solution.

  5. RULE 05

    Kill the phantom four.

    Four things put air and osmotic load into you while contributing almost nothing you actually wanted: carbonation, sugar alcohols, chewing gum, and drinking through a straw. Sparkling water is literally injected gas. Sorbitol, maltitol, xylitol and friends — in your protein bars, your "sugar-free" syrups, your mints — draw water into the bowel and are fermented enthusiastically. Gum and straws are pure swallowed air.

    All four, gone, for seventy-two hours. For a meaningful number of people this one rule alone is the difference, and they never knew because they never removed all four simultaneously.

  6. RULE 06

    Drink more to hold less.

    This is the most counter-intuitive line in the document and the one clients resist hardest. When you are under-hydrated, your body increases the hormone that tells your kidneys to conserve water. You retain because you are dry. Reliable fluid intake, spread across the day, releases that grip.

    Support it with foods and drinks that gently encourage fluid turnover rather than force it: dandelion leaf tea, hibiscus tea, cucumber, celery, asparagus, watermelon. And be clear about what is not on this list — over-the-counter diuretic pills, "water pills", and detox teas that work by causing diarrhoea. Those are how people end up dehydrated, mineral-depleted, and in a clinic. We are not doing that.

  7. RULE 07

    The cosmetic finisher.

    Three small levers that disproportionately change how you photograph on day three. Two kiwifruit in the evening — genuinely one of the better-evidenced whole-food interventions for sluggish bowels, and gentler than most supplements. Magnesium in a well-tolerated form, evening, which supports sleep and bowel comfort together. Zero alcohol — it is a triple hit of dehydration, disrupted sleep, and gut irritation, and it will single-handedly undo the other six rules.

    Then, and only on day three: return to a normal, moderate carbohydrate intake at your final meal. After three lean days, that refill draws water back into the muscle, where fullness reads as tight rather than soft. This is the oldest trick in physique preparation and it is the reason day three is the day you take the photograph.

Before you touch the potassium rule Potassium is not universally safe to increase. If you have kidney disease, or take an ACE inhibitor, an ARB, a potassium-sparing diuretic, or a potassium supplement, do not raise your potassium intake without clearing it with your prescriber first. Same for magnesium if you have reduced kidney function. This is not a formality — it is the one part of this protocol that is genuinely contraindicated for a real group of people.
03PAGE THREE

Training the gut like a nervous system

Your digestive tract has its own dense network of neurons and a permanent open line to your brain. That line is trainable. Most people are running it in the wrong mode all day and wondering why nothing moves.

Digestion is a parasympathetic activity — it happens in the state your body treats as safe. Eat standing at the counter, scrolling, in traffic, mid-argument, on four hours of sleep, and you have asked your gut to run its most demanding process while your physiology is set to alert. It complies badly. Then you blame the food.

The main cable between brain and gut is the vagus nerve, and vagal tone behaves like a trainable quality. You cannot lift it with willpower, but you can reach it directly through breath, through cold, through the muscles of the throat, and through the simple act of slowing down before you eat. Three days is enough to feel a difference. Here is exactly how.

Drill 01 — Breath

Six breaths a minute, five minutes, before every meal

Slow nasal inhale for roughly four counts, long unforced exhale for roughly six. The exhale is where the effect lives: a longer exhale than inhale shifts you toward the rest-and-digest state. Do it seated, eyes soft, immediately before you eat.

CuePlate down → five minutes of breath → first bite.

Drill 02 — Throat

Hum, gargle, or sing for two minutes daily

The vagus nerve innervates the muscles of the larynx and pharynx, which is why humming, gargling water loudly, and singing are used as practical vagal stimulation drills. It feels ridiculous. Do it in the shower where nobody can assess you.

CueShower on → sixty seconds gargling → sixty seconds humming.

Drill 03 — Cold

Thirty seconds of cold on the face

Cold water across the face and the sides of the neck triggers a fast parasympathetic reflex. Thirty seconds at the sink is enough — no ice bath, no theatre. Use it in the evening or any time you feel wired and tight-bellied.

CueCold splash across face and jaw, thirty seconds, nightly.

Drill 04 — Mechanics

Twenty-five chews. Fork down between bites.

Chewing is the only part of digestion under your conscious control, and on a slowed stomach it matters far more than usual — you are pre-processing food that will sit longer. Counting to twenty-five sounds absurd until you realise most people chew six times and swallow.

CueBite → fork down → chew to twenty-five → swallow → pick fork up.

Ninety minutes with nothing in your mouth is not deprivation. It is the only window in which your gut is allowed to clean itself. Every unplanned snack, every coffee with cream, resets that clock to zero.

On the cleanout wave

The clean gaps

Between meals — and only when you are genuinely empty — your small intestine runs a sweeping contraction pattern that clears residue and bacteria downstream. Any calorie interrupts it. Not "a big snack" — any calorie. The splash of oat milk. The three almonds. The taste while cooking.

So the instruction is simple and strict: ninety to one hundred and twenty minutes of completely clean gap between anything caloric. Water, black coffee, and plain tea are fine. Nothing else. Most people eat four to six times a day plus untracked grazing and have not given that system a clear run in years.

Reading your own fullness again

GLP-1 medication turns the fullness signal up loud. That is useful — and it is also why some people stop noticing the difference between satisfied and uncomfortably full, then assume the dose isn't working. Rebuild the scale deliberately: rate every meal 0 to 10 and stop at 6 or 7, where you are content and could still move well. Three days of honest rating restores a sense most people lost long before they started medication.

Sleep is a gut intervention

Short and irregular sleep degrades the intestinal barrier, disrupts the daily rhythm your gut microbes run on, and pushes hunger hormones in exactly the wrong direction. For these three days, the target is not "eight hours" — it is a fixed wake time, within a thirty-minute window, with real daylight in your eyes inside the first half hour. Motility is a rhythm. Rhythms need an anchor.

Stop and get medical care Severe or persistent abdominal pain — especially pain that bores through to your back — relentless vomiting, an inability to keep fluids down, blood in your stool, black tarry stools, no bowel movement at all for several days with pain and distension, signs of significant dehydration, or symptoms of low blood sugar if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea. These are not protocol problems. Stop, and contact your prescriber or emergency services.

Behaviour design and the reverse play

The protocol above is the easy half. Getting a human to run it for three consecutive days is the actual work — and it is a design problem, not a discipline problem.

I am going to show you the psychology I am using on you, while I am using it. That is deliberate, and it is not a weakness in the method. Techniques like these keep working when disclosed — a commitment device still costs you something to break even after you have read the paper explaining commitment devices. What disclosure changes is the relationship: it is the line between coaching someone and running a play on them. I would rather you know.

  1. PLAY 01

    Change the noun, not the verb.

    "I'm trying to cut back on late-night eating" is a verb. Verbs are negotiable at 11pm. "I don't eat inside four hours of bed" is a noun — an identity statement — and identity is far more stable under pressure than intention. For seventy-two hours you are not attempting anything. You are a person who finishes eating at a certain hour. Say it out loud once. It is embarrassing and it works.

  2. PLAY 02

    Write the if-then before you need it.

    Decisions made in advance, in a calm state, get executed. Decisions left to the moment get renegotiated. So write three specific if-then plans now, on paper: If someone offers me a drink, then I take sparkling water with lime and I don't explain myself. If I finish dinner and want something sweet, then I brush my teeth immediately. If I wake up puffy on day two, then I take the walk anyway. Specificity is the whole active ingredient — vague intentions fail at the exact moment you needed them.

  3. PLAY 03

    Move the object, not your willpower.

    Every gram of willpower you spend resisting something in your own kitchen is a gram you didn't have for anything else. So change the kitchen. The sparkling water goes out of the house for three days. The gum leaves the bag. The kiwifruit sits at eye level in the fridge. The walking shoes go by the door before you go to bed. You are not weak — you have just been fighting an environment that was engineered against you.

  4. PLAY 04

    Make quitting cost something real.

    Tell one specific person — by name, today — that you are running a seventy-two hour protocol and that you will report your numbers to them on the third night. Not "I'm being good this week." The specific claim, to a specific human, with a specific deadline. Losing something you already have is a stronger motivator than gaining something you don't, and once you have said it out loud, your reputation with that person is something you already have.

  5. PLAY 05

    Leave the loop open.

    Unfinished things occupy your mind in a way that finished things do not — a completed task falls out of memory almost immediately, while an open one keeps surfacing uninvited. This protocol is engineered to stay open. There is a measurement at hour zero and a measurement at hour seventy-two, and until that second number exists the whole thing is unresolved. You will find yourself thinking about it on day two whether you want to or not. That is not an accident and it is the reason it gets finished.

The ban list — read carefully

Here is the reverse play, stated plainly. Everything below is forbidden for seventy-two hours. Not discouraged — forbidden. You are going to feel a flicker of irritation at being told what you can't do, and that flicker is exactly the fuel I want, pointed at the five things that most reliably wreck people in the first three days.

  1. You may not weigh yourself on day two. Hour zero and hour seventy-two only. Day two is the day water shifts make the number lie to you, and the day most people quit over a lie.
  2. You may not add anything to this protocol. No extra supplement, no fasting on top, no doubling the fibre, no "I'll also do cardio twice a day." Additions are how you turn a clean signal into noise you can't read.
  3. You may not skip the walks because the food was perfect. The walk is not a calorie tax. It is a motility drill. Perfect eating with zero movement is a slower gut, not a faster one.
  4. You may not run a fourth day. Seventy-two hours, then you stop and report. You do not get day four by wanting it. You get it by finishing this one and telling me what happened.
  5. You may not tell me it "kind of worked." Bring numbers. Waist at the navel, morning weight, bloat score out of ten, and how your rings and shoes fit. Vague reports get vague coaching, and you did not do three days of work for vague coaching.

Notice what just happened. Five minutes ago the walk was a chore. Now it is the thing you have been told you cannot skip — and some part of you wants to do it precisely because of that. Autonomy is the strongest fuel you have. I just handed you the tank.
