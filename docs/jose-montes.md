# Jose Montes — Central Coast

A luxury estate site built as a scroll-linked film: a holographic house that
assembles itself as you scroll, cinematic property plates, the real monthly
numbers behind every asking price, a concierge who answers out loud, and — if
you want it — scrolling with your hand through the camera.

It lives at [`public/jose-montes/`](../public/jose-montes) and shares nothing
with the Cesium globe app but the repository and the dark instrument
aesthetic. No build step, no framework, no dependencies, no backend, no
account. Locally it is `/jose-montes/`
(`http://localhost:4173/jose-montes/` under `./start.sh`).

```sh
npm run test:realtor   # 41 unit tests, no browser needed
npm run qa:realtor     # 21 end-to-end checks driving the real page in Chromium
npm run qa:realtor -- --out shot.png   # …and a screenshot
```

---

## What is actually in it

### One number drives everything

Every motion on the page is a function of the scroll offset. Nothing is
animated on a timer, nothing plays once and stops, and there is no animation
state to unwind — which means scrubbing back up the page runs every shot
backwards exactly, at whatever speed the reader chooses.

The maths is in [`js/scroll.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/scroll.js) and is
pure arithmetic over rectangles:

- **Document progress** fills the rail at the top of the window and lights the
  current chapter.
- **Pinning** is a tall section with a `position: sticky` stage inside it. The
  stage holds still while the extra height scrolls past, and the fraction of
  that height consumed is the scene's timeline, `t`.
- **Reveal and parallax** are an element's position in the viewport,
  normalised, eased, and multiplied by a depth.

[`js/reveal.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/reveal.js) is the only part that
touches the document. It reads every rectangle first and writes every result
second, so the browser never reflows mid-frame, and it writes **custom
properties** rather than transforms: an element is told `--reveal`, `--shift`
or `--t`, and the stylesheet decides what that means. The choreography stays
in CSS, where a designer can change what a number does without touching
JavaScript.

Three scenes are pinned. The hero builds the house. The signature listing
opens its plate like a shutter and slides its panel in on a later beat. The
interiors reel travels sideways while the page travels down.

### The hologram

A hand-written WebGL2 scene in [`js/stage.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/stage.js):
a wireframe estate — slab, terrace, two floors, a cantilevered roof, an
infinity pool and a row of olive trees — standing over a wireframe ocean,
ringed with instrument ticks, lit by drifting motes.

Every vertex is generated by
[`js/geometry.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/geometry.js) from about a hundred
numbers in metres. There are no model files and nothing to download to draw
the house.

Each edge carries an **assembly order** in the range 0–1 alongside its
position. The vertex shader draws an edge only once the scene's build progress
has passed its order, with a bright crest at the leading edge, so the estate
draws itself from the foundations up as you scroll and un-draws itself as you
scroll back. That is one float per vertex and no CPU work at all.

Two shader programs do all of it — additive lines and additive point sprites —
so the whole renderer is one file and one draw list. If WebGL2 is missing,
`Stage.start()` returns false and the page keeps a CSS gradient behind the
hero; nothing else changes.

### The concierge

She has a mind and a mouth, and they are separate files.

[`js/concierge.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/concierge.js) is the mind: two
pure functions and a grammar. `parseRequest` turns an utterance into an
intent; `respond` turns that intent into words plus, where the request implies
one, an action from a small closed vocabulary — `goto`, `focus`, `scroll`,
`audio`, `gesture`, `stop`. It touches no DOM, no microphone and no network,
which is why the whole receptionist is tested from Node.

She answers from the portfolio and the mortgage module rather than from a
script, so the figure she says out loud is the figure on the page. Ask what
the payment is on 123 Ocean View and she runs the same
`ownershipCost` the calculator runs. Ask what you can afford at eight thousand
a month and she inverts it. Ask for the most expensive one and she sorts the
portfolio, says the address, and scrolls its card into view.

A question that names a property is about that property — "how much is 8 Bluff
Trail" is a price question *and* a reference, and she resolves both.

[`js/voice.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/voice.js) is the mouth and the ears:
platform speech synthesis and platform speech recognition. No cloud service,
no key, nothing recorded or uploaded. Two things stop a synthesised voice
sounding like a satnav, and both are done here — the best installed voice is
chosen rather than the default (which on every platform is the flattest one),
and a line is spoken in clauses with the rate and pitch varied slightly per
clause, so it has a contour instead of a monotone.

Recognition support is uneven — Safari and Chrome yes, Firefox no — so the
typed field beside the microphone is a first-class path, never a fallback.

### Scrolling with your hand

Turn on **✋** and the front camera opens. There is no model and no network
call: a downscaled grayscale frame is differenced against the previous one,
the moving pixels are weighed, and their centre of mass is tracked between
frames. Moving a hand down scrolls down.

The maths is in [`js/motion.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/motion.js) and three
rules make it usable rather than merely clever:

1. **A hand, not a flicker.** Motion has to cover a minimum share of the frame
   before the tracker latches, so a passing shadow does nothing.
2. **A deadzone.** Travel under about one percent of the frame is ignored, so
   a hand held still does not creep the page.
3. **Release on absence.** After a short grace period the tracker forgets
   where the hand was, so the next gesture starts clean instead of teleporting
   the page by the whole frame height.

The camera runs at 160×120 and 24 Hz — the gesture needs a position, not a
portrait — which leaves the renderer its frame budget. The preview is on
screen so you can see exactly what the page can see. No frame is uploaded,
recorded or written anywhere.

### The music

[`js/score.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/score.js) synthesises the score with
Web Audio rather than shipping a file: nothing to download, nothing to
licence, no loop seam to notice on a long visit, and it works offline. It is a
slow four-chord progression on a warm pad through a filter opened and closed
by a very slow LFO, a felt-piano voice placing notes from the chord at
unhurried intervals, and a filtered noise bed standing in for surf. The
reverb is a two-second impulse response built from decaying noise at runtime.

It ducks under the concierge, so she is never talking over music.

Everything is gated behind the entry gate, because browsers require a gesture
before audio and because a page that makes noise unasked is a page people
close.

### The numbers

Price is the headline; the payment is what people decide on.
[`js/finance.js`](../public/jose-montes/js/finance.js) computes the whole
monthly cost — loan, county property tax at the San Luis Obispo effective
rate, hazard insurance, and mortgage insurance while the loan is above eighty
percent — because leaving the last three off is how a monthly figure ends up
thirty percent light.

Affordability is the same function inverted by bisection rather than solved
analytically: tax, insurance and PMI all move with the price, and bisection
over a bounded range cannot be wrong about the shape of the answer.

The equity projection separates the two things that build it, because early on
it is almost entirely appreciation and almost none of it is the loan being
paid down.

None of it is a quote and the page says so.

## The pictures

Ten cinematic plates, generated for this site, plus two photographs of Jose.

Each plate ships twice. The committed AVIF is small — the whole set is 88 KB —
and paints instantly, works offline, and is what the page actually ships. The
full-resolution original is a network-only **upgrade**, swapped in by
`upgradePlates()` only after it has decoded, and skipped entirely on a metered
or 2G connection. A blocked or slow network can never leave a hole where a
photograph should be; it just leaves the small one on screen.

## Offline, and on a phone

The service worker caches the whole shell — markup, styles, every module,
every plate — so a second visit works with the signal off. There is nothing
else to fetch: the hologram, the concierge, the music and the maths all run
locally. The full-resolution plates are the one exception and are treated as
network-only, exactly as above.

On a phone the hologram leans with the gyroscope, the field of view widens so
the estate still fits, the mote count drops, and the pixel ratio is capped —
fill rate, not geometry, is what costs a phone its frame budget.

`prefers-reduced-motion` is honoured throughout: reveals resolve to their
final state, the pinned scenes stop transforming, the reel becomes an ordinary
horizontal scroller, and smooth scrolling is turned off.

## Design notes

**Why custom properties rather than inline transforms.** An element that is
told its progress can respond to it in a completely different way — the hero
plate scales and desaturates, the signature plate opens like a shutter, the
reel translates sideways — without a line of JavaScript changing. It also
means the whole choreography is inspectable in dev tools.

**Why the assembly order lives in the vertex buffer.** The alternative is
uploading new geometry as the scene builds, or drawing a growing subset of the
index buffer. One float per vertex and a comparison in the shader is cheaper
than either and is exactly reversible.

**Why the concierge is two files.** A grammar that can only be tested by
opening a browser and talking to it does not get tested. Splitting the mind
from the mouth means every intent, every reply and every action is asserted in
Node in under a tenth of a second.

**Why bisection for affordability.** Solving the cost function backwards is
possible but brittle — and a brittle formula that is subtly wrong about PMI is
worse than a loop that is obviously right.

**Why the transcript rests.** A chat window that stays open covers the page.
The console fades back eleven seconds after the last line and returns on hover
or focus.

## Known limits

- **Speech recognition** is Safari and Chrome only. Firefox users type; the
  concierge is identical either way.
- **Speech synthesis quality varies by platform.** The voice ranking picks the
  best installed voice, but a device with only compact voices will sound like
  a device with only compact voices.
- **The gesture tracker follows movement, not hands.** It cannot tell a hand
  from a passing cat. The energy threshold and the deadzone keep the common
  cases sane; a busy background behind you will still confuse it.
- **The listings, the figures and the track record are illustrative.** This is
  a demonstration site, and the footer says so.
- **The contact form keeps requests in `localStorage`.** There is no backend,
  and the form says that too.
