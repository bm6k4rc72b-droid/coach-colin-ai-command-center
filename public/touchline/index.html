      What this cannot do

      • Recognise a player. No shirt numbers are read, no faces are matched. Sides come from kit colour and nothing else, and a player who walks out of frame returns as a different label with an empty history.
      • Predict whether a pass would be completed. Lanes are measured — length, the nearest defender's clearance, whether it is closing. There is no completion percentage, because that needs thousands of labelled passes from footage like yours, and a number invented from geometry and printed as a percentage is a guess in a trustworthy typeface.
      • Reconstruct a match in 3D. One camera on a plane gives positions on the grass. It cannot give height, so a header, a chipped pass and a shot over the bar are the same to it.
      • Follow a moving camera. A homography is fitted to one view. Small nudges are compensated; a pan or a zoom kills it, and the app says the metres are stale rather than reporting a pitch full of players sprinting sideways at once.
      • See the ball reliably. A ball is 22 cm, which is one or two pixels at the far end of a pitch, and it spends much of the match under a player. Possession is only counted while it is genuinely visible, and the share of time that could not be attributed is printed next to the result.
      • Report a full-match total from partial tracking. Every player row carries the fraction of the session they were tracked for. Nothing is scaled up to ninety minutes.
