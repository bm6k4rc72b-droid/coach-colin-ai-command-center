How it works

The detector

Each frame is white-balanced, then every pixel is projected onto the crop's ripening colour path — the ordered hue anchors that a fruit of that species passes through from immature to ripe. Pixels close to the path become a fruit mask; the mask is de-speckled, split into connected blobs, and each blob is scored on shape, colour purity, smoothness and size. Green fruit has to clear a stricter bar, because unripe fruit and foliage share a hue and only texture and roundness tell them apart.

The forecast

With no history, days-to-harvest comes from the crop's nominal cycle length, warped for temperature. Once a block has been scanned at least twice more than half a day apart, a regression through its own readings replaces the guess, and the readout says measured instead of nominal. That is the whole reason to log scans.

Honest limits

  • This is a colour and shape estimator, not a trained neural network. It does not know what a tomato is; it knows what ripening looks like in hue space.
  • It cannot see fruit hidden behind leaves, so counts are what is visible, not what is on the plant.
  • Internal quality — sugar, acidity, firmness — is not visible to a camera. Colour leads and correlates; it does not replace a refractometer.
  • Mixed or harsh lighting shifts hue. Calibrate on something neutral before trusting numbers across a whole day.
  • Anthocyanin crops (aubergine, dark grapes) and blushed cultivars benefit most from teach mode.

Privacy

Frames are analysed in memory and discarded. Nothing is uploaded — there is no server to upload to. Scans, settings and taught colours live in this browser's local storage and leave only when you export them.
