How it works

The detector

Each frame is white-balanced, then every pixel is projected onto the crop's ripening colour path — the ordered hue anchors that a fruit of that species passes through from immature to ripe. Pixels close to the path become a fruit mask; the mask is de-speckled, split into connected blobs, and each blob is scored on shape, colour purity, smoothness and size. Green fruit has to clear a stricter bar, because unripe fruit and foliage share a hue and only texture and roundness tell them apart.

The forecast

With no history, days-to-harvest comes from the crop's nominal cycle length, warped for temperature. Once a block has been scanned at least twice more than half a day apart, a regression through its own readings replaces the guess, and the readout says measured instead of nominal. That is the whole reason to log scans.

Canopy mode

The second mode stops looking for fruit and reads the leaves instead. Each pixel is scored with a published visible-band vegetation index, masked to plant pixels, and reduced to zone statistics — the same arithmetic behind the false-colour maps you see from agricultural drones.

What it can tell you: canopy cover, vigour, how much of the block is yellowing (consistent with nitrogen shortfall), how much leaf area is necrotic, and — most usefully — how far the weak zones sit below the strongest zones of the same field. That last number needs no calibration, because the field is its own reference.

What it is not: hyperspectral imaging. A hyperspectral camera records a hundred or more narrow bands per pixel; your phone records three broad, overlapping ones. There is no way to recover a spectral cube from that, and any app claiming to is showing you a picture, not a measurement.

NDVI is defined on near-infrared, and every phone has an IR-cut filter bonded over the sensor, so NDVI stays greyed out until you tell the app you have attached an IR-converted camera. A conversion is genuinely cheap, and with one the app computes real NDVI — nothing simulated.

Indices disagree, deliberately. NGRDI and GLI react strongly to a canopy thinning or yellowing; TGI tracks chlorophyll and can sit almost unmoved by a stress the others shout about. Switching index when a result looks wrong is using the tool correctly, not doubting it.

Honest limits

  • This is a colour and shape estimator, not a trained neural network. It does not know what a tomato is; it knows what ripening looks like in hue space.
  • It cannot see fruit hidden behind leaves, so counts are what is visible, not what is on the plant.
  • Internal quality — sugar, acidity, firmness — is not visible to a camera. Colour leads and correlates; it does not replace a refractometer.
  • Mixed or harsh lighting shifts hue. Calibrate on something neutral before trusting numbers across a whole day.
  • Anthocyanin crops (aubergine, dark grapes) and blushed cultivars benefit most from teach mode.
  • Vegetation indices are relative, not absolute. A TGI of 0.3 is poor in one crop and excellent in another — compare a block to itself over time, or to the rest of the same field, never to a number off the internet.
  • Water stress is not directly visible to any colour camera. Wilting and colour loss are late, indirect symptoms; measuring water stress properly needs canopy temperature from a thermal sensor.

Privacy

Frames are analysed in memory and discarded. Nothing is uploaded — there is no server to upload to. Scans, settings and taught colours live in this browser's local storage and leave only when you export them.
