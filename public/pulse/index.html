How it works

Every heartbeat pushes a pulse of blood through the capillaries just under your skin. That extra blood absorbs slightly more light, so the colour of your skin flickers a few parts per thousand in time with your pulse — far too faint to see, but not too faint for a camera sensor to measure.

  1. Sample. Each frame is reduced to the average colour of one small region — your forehead, or your fingertip.
  2. Project. Those red, green and blue traces are combined with the POS chrominance method, which cancels the colour changes caused by movement and shifting light.
  3. Analyse. The result is band-limited to 42–240 BPM and transformed into a spectrum; the strongest peak is your pulse rate.
  4. Score. Confidence comes from how much of the band's energy sits in that peak and its first harmonic. A weak peak reports low confidence rather than a made-up number.

Getting a good reading

  • Hold still. Motion is the single biggest source of error.
  • Use soft, even lighting on your face. Avoid backlighting and flicker from some LED bulbs.
  • Give it at least 10–15 seconds; the estimate settles as the window fills.
  • If face mode struggles, try fingertip mode: cover the rear camera and its torch with one fingertip, resting lightly.

Privacy

Video never leaves your device. There is no server, no upload and no analytics — frames are read into memory, reduced to a handful of numbers, and discarded. Saved readings live in this browser's local storage until you clear them.

Limits

Accuracy degrades with movement, low light, heavy makeup, and rapid rate changes. The variability figures are indicative only: camera timing is far coarser than an ECG. This tool cannot measure blood pressure, oxygen saturation or any other vital sign, and it is not a medical device.
